The Book of Mozilla: Firefox's Hidden Apocalyptic Verses

"The Book of Mozilla" is a famous software Easter egg found in the Netscape and Mozilla series of web browsers, most notably Mozilla Firefox. This article provides a complete breakdown of what this hidden feature is, the real-world browser history concealed behind its metaphors, and the exact apocalyptic verses that appear when you type about:mozilla into the Firefox address bar.

What is The Book of Mozilla?

There is no actual book titled "The Book of Mozilla." Instead, it is a series of hidden pages built into the browser's code. When a user types about:mozilla into the Firefox address bar and presses Enter, the browser displays a maroon screen with white text containing a single quote.

These quotes are written in an apocalyptic tone that mimics the biblical Book of Revelation. In these verses, the "Beast" is a metaphor for Netscape and later the Mozilla project—a nod to Netscape's original green lizard mascot, which was also named Mozilla. The "followers of Mammon" typically refer to profit-driven competitors, originally Microsoft's Internet Explorer and later tech giants like Apple and Google.

Instead of referencing actual chapters and verses, the numbers at the bottom of each passage represent significant dates (Month:Day) in the history of the Netscape and Mozilla browsers. As the browser has updated over the decades, the verse displayed by about:mozilla has changed to reflect the current era of the company's history.

The Apocalyptic Verses

Below are the official verses of The Book of Mozilla that have appeared in Netscape and Firefox over the years, presented in chronological order alongside their historical meanings.

The Book of Mozilla, 12:10

"And the beast shall come forth surrounded by a roiling cloud of vengeance. The house of the unbelievers shall be razed and they shall be scorched to the earth. Their tags shall blink until the end of days."

  • Date: December 10, 1994
  • Meaning: This date marks the release of Netscape Navigator 1.0. The verse jokingly threatens "unbelievers" with the <blink> HTML tag, a controversial and annoying text effect introduced by Netscape.

The Book of Mozilla, 3:31

"And the beast shall be made legion. Its numbers shall be increased a thousand thousand fold. The din of a million keyboards like unto a great storm shall cover the earth, and the followers of Mammon shall tremble."

  • Date: March 31, 1998
  • Meaning: This was the day Netscape released its source code to the public, launching the open-source Mozilla project. Making the beast "legion" refers to the thousands of open-source developers around the world ("a million keyboards") who could now help build the browser to fight "Mammon" (Microsoft Internet Explorer).

The Book of Mozilla, 7:15

"And so at last the beast fell and the unbelievers rejoiced. But all was not lost, for from the ash rose a great bird. The bird gazed down upon the unbelievers and cast fire and thunder upon them. For the beast had been reborn with its strength renewed, and the followers of Mammon cowered in horror."

  • Date: July 15, 2003
  • Meaning: On this day, America Online (AOL) shut down the Netscape browser division (the beast fell), and the non-profit Mozilla Foundation was launched. The "great bird" that cast "fire and thunder" refers to the Mozilla Firebird browser (later renamed Firefox) and the Mozilla Thunderbird email client.

The Book of Mozilla, 8:20

"And the creator of the beast forged a new name for it, and the beast was made strong. It went forth and multiplied, and brought forth a new generation of helpers to stand beside it in the battle against the followers of Mammon."

  • Date: August 20, 2006
  • Meaning: This verse only appeared in Netscape Navigator 9. It marks the date the first internal email was sent proposing the development of Netscape 9, reviving the browser one last time before its permanent discontinuation.

The Book of Mozilla, 11:9

"Mammon slept. And the beast reborn spread over the earth and its numbers grew legion. And they proclaimed the times and sacrificed crops unto the fire, with the cunning of foxes. And they built a new world in their own image as promised by the sacred words, and spoke of the beast with their children. Mammon awoke, and lo! it was naught but a follower."

  • Date: November 9, 2004
  • Meaning: This celebrates the release of Firefox 1.0. "Mammon slept" refers to the five-year period where Microsoft failed to update Internet Explorer. "Sacrificed crops" and "proclaimed the times" refer to fans creating a giant Firefox logo crop circle and taking out a full-page advertisement in The New York Times.

The Book of Mozilla, 15:1

"The twins of Mammon quarrelled. Their warring plunged the world into a new darkness, and the beast abhorred the darkness. So it began to move swiftly, and grew more powerful, and went forth and multiplied. And the beasts brought fire and light to the darkness."

  • Date: January 15, 2013
  • Meaning: Note the European date format (Day:Month). The "twins of Mammon" represent Apple (iOS) and Google (Android) dominating the mobile operating system market. "Move swiftly" refers to Firefox switching to a rapid release cycle, and the verse commemorates the announcement of the first Firefox OS smartphones.

The Book of Mozilla, 11:14

"The Beast adopted new raiment and studied the ways of Time and Space and Light and the Flow of energy through the Universe. From its studies, the Beast fashioned new structures from oxidised metal and proclaimed their glories. And the Beast's followers rejoiced, finding renewed purpose in these teachings."

  • Date: November 14, 2017
  • Meaning: This marks the release of Firefox 57, codenamed "Firefox Quantum." "Time and Space and Light" and "Flow" refer to internal Mozilla projects (Quantum Flow, Photon UI) that redesigned the browser. "Oxidised metal" is a direct reference to Rust, the programming language developed by Mozilla to build the new browser engine.

The Book of Mozilla, 6:27

"The Beast continued its studies with renewed Focus, building great Reference works and contemplating new Realities. The Beast brought forth its followers and acolytes to create a renewed smaller form of itself and, through Mischievous means, sent it out across the world."

  • Date: June 27, 2023
  • Meaning: This is the verse found in modern versions of Firefox. The capitalized words refer to specific Mozilla projects: Firefox Focus (a privacy-centric mobile browser), Reference Browser (an open-source test browser), Firefox Reality (a virtual reality browser), and Fenix (the "renewed smaller form" codename for the rebuilt Android browser). "Mischievous means" refers to Loki, the codename for one of Mozilla's internal initiatives.
