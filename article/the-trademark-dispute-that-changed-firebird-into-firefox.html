The Trademark Dispute That Changed Firebird into Firefox

Before becoming the globally recognized Firefox web browser, Mozilla's flagship project underwent two major name changes due to trademark conflicts. Initially called Phoenix, the browser was renamed to Firebird in 2003, which immediately sparked a heated dispute with an existing open-source database project of the same name. Ultimately, intense pressure from the open-source community forced Mozilla to abandon the Firebird name in 2004, leading to the birth of the Firefox brand.

The naming trouble began in 2002 when Mozilla launched the browser as "Phoenix." That name was quickly dropped after Phoenix Technologies, a well-known BIOS manufacturer, asserted its trademark rights. In April 2003, Mozilla announced the browser's new name: Firebird.

Almost immediately, a new conflict arose. The name "Firebird" was already in use by the Firebird Project, an open-source relational database management system that had spun off from Borland's InterBase in 2000.

Because both the browser and the database were free, open-source software programs, the database developers and their users were alarmed. They argued that Mozilla's massive popularity would overshadow their database project, confuse users searching for "Firebird" software online, and dilute their established brand within the open-source ecosystem.

Initially, the Mozilla Foundation resisted changing the name again. Within days of the backlash, Mozilla issued a statement declaring that the browser should always be referred to as "Mozilla Firebird" to avoid confusion with the "Firebird SQL" database.

However, this compromise failed. Tech journalists, developers, and everyday users simply called the browser "Firebird." The database community remained unsatisfied and continued a vocal campaign against Mozilla, accusing the foundation of bullying a smaller open-source project.

Recognizing that the dispute was damaging its reputation within the open-source community and creating potential legal hurdles for future commercial distribution, Mozilla relented. On February 9, 2004, Mozilla officially abandoned the Firebird moniker and renamed the browser "Mozilla Firefox."

This time, the Mozilla Foundation conducted thorough trademark clearance before making the announcement, registering the Firefox name with the U.S. Patent and Trademark Office and securing permission from the UK-based Charlton Company, which already held a software trademark for the name in the United Kingdom.
