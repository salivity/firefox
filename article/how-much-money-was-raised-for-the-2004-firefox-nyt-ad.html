How Much Money Was Raised for the 2004 Firefox NYT Ad?

This article explores the historic 2004 crowdfunding campaign organized by the Mozilla Foundation to promote the launch of the Firefox 1.0 web browser. In just ten days, approximately 10,000 community members banded together and raised roughly $250,000 to purchase a two-page advertisement in The New York Times, marking a major milestone in both open-source software marketing and early internet crowdfunding.

The fundraising drive began in October 2004 through the "Spread Firefox" marketing portal. Mozilla initially asked its community for small donations, typically ranging from $10 to $30, with the goal of funding a single-page print advertisement to celebrate the upcoming November release of Firefox 1.0.

The community response vastly exceeded Mozilla's expectations. Within a strict ten-day window, around 10,000 individual donors contributed a total of $250,000. Because the campaign brought in significantly more money than a one-page ad required, Mozilla upgraded the purchase to a massive two-page spread.

The advertisement officially ran in the December 16, 2004 edition of The New York Times. To honor the grassroots nature of the funding, the two-page layout featured the Firefox logo alongside the names of every single person who contributed to the $250,000 fund printed in tiny font across the pages.

This $250,000 campaign was a pivotal moment in tech history. At the time, Microsoft's Internet Explorer held an overwhelming monopoly on the web browser market. By funding a high-profile advertisement in a major publication entirely through user donations, the Firefox community proved that an open-source project could successfully challenge a tech giant through collective consumer support.
