How the Mozilla Foundation and Corporation Govern Firefox

This article explores the unique governance model behind the Firefox web browser, which relies on a symbiotic relationship between the non-profit Mozilla Foundation and its wholly-owned taxable subsidiary, the Mozilla Corporation. By dividing responsibilities between mission-driven advocacy and commercial product development, this dual structure allows Firefox to compete in the modern tech landscape while remaining strictly committed to open-source principles, user privacy, and an open internet.

The Dual Structure Explained

To understand how Firefox is governed, it is necessary to distinguish between the two entities that oversee it.

The Mozilla Foundation, established in 2003, is a 501(c)(3) non-profit organization. Its primary purpose is to promote openness, innovation, and participation on the internet. Because of its tax-exempt status, the Foundation is legally restricted in how much commercial activity it can conduct and how much revenue it can generate from business partnerships.

To bypass these limitations without sacrificing its core values, the Foundation created the Mozilla Corporation in 2005. The Corporation is a fully taxable, for-profit entity that is 100% owned by the non-profit Foundation.

The Role of the Mozilla Foundation: Mission and Ultimate Control

The Mozilla Foundation acts as the moral compass and ultimate authority over Firefox. It governs the browser through several key mechanisms:

  • The Mozilla Manifesto: The Foundation authors and upholds the Mozilla Manifesto, a set of ten principles that dictate how internet technology should respect human dignity, privacy, and security. Every decision made regarding Firefox must align with these principles.
  • Sole Ownership: Unlike traditional tech companies that answer to public shareholders or venture capitalists demanding maximum profit, the Mozilla Corporation has only one shareholder: the Mozilla Foundation.
  • Board Oversight: The Foundation’s Board of Directors controls the Corporation’s Board of Directors. If the Corporation were ever to steer Firefox in a direction that prioritized profits over user privacy, the Foundation has the legal authority to intervene and replace the Corporation's leadership.
  • Intellectual Property: The Foundation ultimately stewards the open-source licenses and core philosophies that keep Firefox’s underlying source code free and available to the public.

The Role of the Mozilla Corporation: Execution and Revenue

While the Foundation sets the rules, the Mozilla Corporation handles the day-to-day governance and operations of the Firefox browser. Its responsibilities include:

  • Engineering and Development: The Corporation employs the developers, designers, and security experts who write the code, fix bugs, and release updates for Firefox.
  • Commercial Partnerships: Running a modern web browser requires hundreds of millions of dollars annually. The Corporation negotiates lucrative business deals—most notably search engine royalties where companies like Google pay to be the default search provider in Firefox.
  • Marketing and Distribution: The Corporation manages the Firefox brand, coordinates global marketing campaigns, and develops supplementary commercial products, such as Mozilla VPN, to diversify its revenue streams.
  • Talent Acquisition: Operating as a corporation allows Mozilla to offer competitive salaries and benefits to attract top-tier engineering and executive talent from the broader tech industry, something a traditional charity would struggle to justify under strict non-profit regulations.

The Financial and Governance Feedback Loop

The governance of Firefox relies on a continuous feedback loop between the two organizations. The Mozilla Corporation uses Firefox to generate revenue through search partnerships and product subscriptions. It uses the majority of this money to fund the ongoing development of the browser and pay its employees.

A portion of the Corporation's profits is also funneled back up to the Mozilla Foundation via dividends and royalties. The Foundation then uses these funds to support broader internet health initiatives, grants, and advocacy programs.

How This Relationship Impacts Firefox Users

For the end user, this governance model directly dictates how Firefox functions as a product. Because the non-profit Foundation holds ultimate control, the Corporation is shielded from the market pressures that force other browser makers to monetize user data.

When Firefox product managers decide which features to build, their mandate is not to maximize user screen time or harvest personal data for targeted advertising. Instead, the Corporation is governed to build features like Enhanced Tracking Protection, which actively blocks the commercial surveillance models used by its competitors. In short, the Corporation provides the financial and operational muscle to keep Firefox competitive, while the Foundation ensures the browser remains a public resource rather than a vehicle for corporate profit.
