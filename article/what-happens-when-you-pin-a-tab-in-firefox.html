What Happens When You Pin a Tab in Firefox?

This article provides a quick overview of how pinning a tab in the Mozilla Firefox web browser changes its appearance and behavior. Specifically, it covers the immediate modifications made to the tab's size, its position on the browser's tab bar, and the status of its close button.

Tab Size

When you pin a tab in Firefox, its size shrinks significantly. The text showing the webpage's title is completely hidden, and the tab is reduced to a small, compact square that only displays the website's favicon (the site's official icon). This reduction in size allows you to keep multiple essential websites open at once without taking up valuable space on your tab bar.

Tab Position

Upon pinning, the tab automatically moves to the far-left side of the tab bar. It locks into this area, sitting to the left of all your standard, unpinned tabs. If you pin multiple tabs, they will group together on the left side. While you can click and drag pinned tabs to rearrange their order among other pinned tabs, you cannot move them to the right of your regular tabs. Furthermore, if you open enough regular tabs to make the tab bar scroll, your pinned tabs remain permanently fixed on the left and will never scroll out of view.

The Close Button

Pinning a tab completely removes the "X" close button from the tab. Firefox hides this button to prevent you from accidentally closing a webpage that you intend to keep open for long-term use. If you decide you want to close a pinned tab, you must right-click on the tab and select "Close Tab" from the context menu, or use the keyboard shortcut Ctrl + W (Cmd + W on a Mac) while the pinned tab is actively selected.
