The Release Year of Mozilla Firefox Version 1.0

This article provides a concise look at the official public release of the Mozilla Firefox web browser under version 1.0. It covers the exact year the browser launched, the development history and name changes that preceded it, and how this landmark release successfully challenged the existing web browser monopoly.

Mozilla Firefox version 1.0 was originally released to the public in 2004. The official launch took place on November 9, 2004.

While version 1.0 marked its first major public milestone, the browser's development actually began two years earlier in 2002. Created by Dave Hyatt, Joe Hewitt, and Blake Ross as an experimental branch of the Mozilla Project, the browser went through several beta versions and name changes before hitting version 1.0. It was originally released to testers under the name "Phoenix" and later renamed "Firebird" to avoid trademark disputes. Due to further trademark conflicts with another open-source project, Mozilla finally settled on the name "Firefox" in February 2004 with the release of version 0.8.

When Firefox 1.0 launched in November 2004, its primary goal was to combat the dominance of Microsoft's Internet Explorer, which controlled roughly 90 percent of the browser market at the time. Internet Explorer was widely criticized for its lack of innovation and severe security vulnerabilities.

Firefox 1.0 immediately appealed to the public by offering a faster, safer, and more efficient browsing experience. Key features that drove its rapid adoption included:

  • Tabbed Browsing: Allowed users to open multiple websites within a single window.
  • Pop-up Blocker: Built-in protection against intrusive pop-up advertisements.
  • Enhanced Security: An architecture designed to prevent malicious scripts from automatically installing spyware on a user's computer.
  • Customizability: Support for user-created extensions, add-ons, and visual themes.

The 2004 release was a massive success. Backed by a passionate community that raised funds to take out a two-page promotional advertisement in The New York Times, Firefox 1.0 saw 25 million downloads within its first 99 days. Less than a year after its November 2004 debut, the browser surpassed 100 million downloads, permanently shifting the landscape of the internet and sparking the modern browser wars.
