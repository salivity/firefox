Data Firefox Private Browsing Stores in RAM and Discards

This article provides a quick overview of the specific types of data that the Mozilla Firefox web browser temporarily holds in your computer's system RAM during a Private Browsing session. By routing this session information to volatile memory instead of writing it to your hard drive or solid-state drive, Firefox ensures that your browsing history, cookies, cached files, and temporary form data are permanently erased the moment you close the Private Browsing window.

When you use a standard browser window, Firefox writes data to your computer's permanent storage drive. In Private Browsing mode, Firefox disables disk writing for session data and instead allocates a portion of your system's Random Access Memory (RAM) to keep the browser functioning normally. Because RAM is volatile memory, clearing the application's memory allocation upon closing the window instantly destroys the data.

Here is the exact data that Firefox temporarily stores in RAM and discards when you close all Private Browsing windows:

Browsing History

Every website address (URL) you visit is recorded in RAM so that the browser's "Back" and "Forward" buttons function properly during your active session. Once the private window is closed, this temporary list of visited pages is wiped from memory and is never added to your permanent Firefox history.

Cookies

Websites use cookies to remember your login states, site preferences, and shopping cart contents. During a Private Browsing session, Firefox accepts cookies so that websites do not break, but it isolates them in a separate, memory-only database. These cookies cannot interact with the cookies from your normal browsing sessions and are destroyed when the private session ends.

Cached Web Content

To make web pages load faster, browsers download and save website assets like images, scripts, and stylesheets. In Private Browsing, Firefox disables its standard disk cache and strictly uses a RAM-based memory cache. All temporary internet files and media assets loaded during your session are immediately purged from RAM upon exit.

Web Storage (DOM Storage)

Modern websites use HTML5 Web Storage—specifically localStorage and sessionStorage—to store larger amounts of data on your machine than traditional cookies allow. Firefox routes all DOM storage requests to system RAM during a private session and deletes the data when the window closes.

Search and Form Entries

Any text you type into the Firefox search bar, the address bar, or text fields on websites (such as names, email addresses, or credit card numbers) is held in RAM while you interact with the page. Firefox will not save these entries to its permanent autocomplete database.

Download List Records

If you download a file during a Private Browsing session, Firefox keeps a temporary record of the transfer in RAM so you can view the download progress and open the file from the browser. When you close the private window, the record of the download is erased from Firefox's download manager. However, the actual file you downloaded is written to your hard drive and will remain on your computer until you manually delete it.

Copy and Paste Buffers (Clipboard)

While not strictly managed by Firefox alone, any text or images you copy to your clipboard during a Private Browsing session are stored in your operating system's RAM. Firefox clears its internal reference to this data upon closing, though the last item you copied may remain in your system's general clipboard until you copy something else or restart your computer.
